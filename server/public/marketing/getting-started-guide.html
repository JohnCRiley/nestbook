Welcome to NestBook

You've just verified your email, and that's genuinely the hardest part done — from here it's just a series of small, sensible steps.

This guide is written for one purpose: so you never have to guess what to do next, and never have to switch between the app and a help page to figure something out. Everything is here, in the order you'll actually need it.

You don't need to be technical to use NestBook, and you don't need to read this guide from cover to cover in one sitting. Work through it at whatever pace suits you — do a step, close the guide, come back to it tomorrow. Nothing here is timed or urgent except the small handful of things we've clearly marked.

How this guide is organised

Each step below matches something you'll actually do inside NestBook, roughly in the order most owners tackle them. Depending on your rental mode — Individual Rooms, Whole Property, or Self-Catering — a few sections won't apply to you; we've noted those clearly as we go.

A calm walk-through of your very first login, so nothing on the screen feels unfamiliar.

Right now it's simply waiting for you to add your property details, your rooms, and your first booking. There is no wrong order. We're about to walk through setup in the order most owners find easiest, but nothing will break if you jump ahead or come back to something later. Every setting can be changed at any time.

One decision to make early: which rental mode fits your property?

Before you go much further, it's worth being clear on which type of property you have, because it changes what a few screens look like. NestBook supports three rental modes:

Individual Rooms (IR mode)
You have several rooms, each bookable separately (a B&B, a small guest house, a hotel-style property). Guests choose a specific room. Within IR mode, you'll also choose how rooms are presented to guests:
Named Rooms — each room has its own name and character (e.g. "The Oak Room"), with its own availability calendar.
Room Categories — rooms are grouped by type (e.g. Single, Double), and guests choose a category first.
Whole Property (WP mode)
Your entire property is booked as one unit by one party at a time (a gîte, a holiday cottage, a lodge, a villa). There's nothing to "choose" — it's simply available or it isn't.
Self-Catering
Your property is made up of several independent self-catering units within one building or site. Choose whichever sub-type fits:
Aparthotel — apartments run like a small hotel, with reception and optional breakfast.
Glamping — pods, cabins or caravans with a central check-in point.
Serviced Apartment — independent apartments with lighter-touch, less daily-facing management.

You'll set this when you fill in your Property Details in the next step, and NestBook will quietly adjust several screens to match — for example, Whole Property and Self-Catering owners won't see room-by-room occupancy stats that wouldn't make sense for them, and won't see a breakfast toggle where guests typically self-cater.

Your property details
The foundation everything else builds on
Go to Settings → Property Details
You've likely already done thisMost of what's below — your property name, type, address, check-in and check-out times, and currency — was probably already filled in during the short setup wizard right after you verified your email. If you completed that, feel free to skip ahead to Step 3. Either way, every one of these details can be changed at any time from Settings — nothing here is permanent.

This is usually the very first thing to fill in, because a handful of other screens (your booking page, your widget, your emails to guests) all pull information from here.

Property name

This is what your guests will see everywhere — on your booking page, in confirmation emails, and on your widget. It's worth taking a moment to get this exactly right, since it also shapes your booking page web address (more on that in Step 7).

Property type

This is where you confirm your rental mode — Individual Rooms, Whole Property, or Self-Catering — as covered in Step 1.

Address

Your full street address. This is used to show a map on your standalone booking page, so guests can see exactly where you are before they book.

Check-in and check-out times

Set the times you actually want guests arriving and leaving. This isn't just informational — NestBook uses it to sensibly guide the app itself. For example, the "Check In" button on your Dashboard won't let a booking be marked as checked in before your configured check-in time has actually arrived, which stops accidental early check-ins being logged by mistake.

Why the check-in time matters more than it looksIf you ever go to check a guest in and the button doesn't seem to work, it's very likely simply because it's before your set check-in time. This is working as intended, not a fault — NestBook will tell you the exact time check-in opens if you try early.

Currency

Set this once, correctly, at the start — it affects every rate, every invoice, and every guest-facing price across the whole system.

Description

A free-text description of your property, shown on your standalone booking page. Write it the way you'd describe your property to a friend — warm and honest tends to work far better than a list of amenities alone.

Properties

If you're on the Multi plan and manage more than one property, this is also where you add and switch between them. Each property has entirely separate rooms, bookings, guests, and settings.

Appearance

NestBook comes with eleven complete colour themes, ranging from calm sage greens through navy, warm gold, ruby and charcoal. Pick whichever one feels most like your property; your booking widget and standalone booking page will automatically match whatever you choose here. You're welcome to change this at any time — nothing is locked in.

Adding your rooms, units, or whole property
Go to Rooms in the main menu

If you're set up as Individual Rooms or Self-Catering, this is where you'll add each room or unit. If you're a Whole Property rental, you'll add a single entry representing the entire property — everything below still applies, just with one listing instead of several.

Already have a list of rooms?If you're moving from another system, you don't have to add each room by hand. Click Import Rooms at the top of the Rooms (or Units) screen and upload a CSV of your rooms, categories, or units. There's an import wizard for each rental mode — Named Rooms, Room Categories, Whole Property, and Self-Catering — and NestBook shows you the right one automatically. Step 11 covers this alongside the booking and guest imports. Otherwise, add each room by hand as below.

Basic details

Give each room or unit a clear name (guests will see this — "Room 3" is functional but "The Garden Room" tends to book better), set its maximum capacity, and add a description. A genuine, specific description usually reassures a guest far more than a generic one.

Base rate

This is the standard nightly price — the price that applies unless a seasonal rate (Step 4) says otherwise. You can always come back and adjust this.

Photos

Upload as many photos as your plan allows — Free properties can upload 3 photos per room, Pro allows 5, and Multi allows 10. Your first photo becomes the "hero" photo shown on your booking page and in the room card on your widget. You can click any thumbnail afterwards to switch which one guests see first.

Once you've got photos in, the Media Library (in the main menu) gives you one place to see and manage every photo across your whole property — room and unit photos, your booking-page hero image, and your logo. From there you can move a photo from one room to another, upload new ones, or copy a direct link to any image.

Photos genuinely matterGuests decide whether to click through to book largely based on your photos, before they've read a single word of your description. It's worth taking a little extra time here — natural light, a tidy room, and a photo that shows the whole space (not just a close-up) all help.

Amenities

Tick whichever amenities genuinely apply — Wi-Fi, en-suite, air conditioning, and so on. These show on your booking page and help a guest quickly judge whether the room suits them.

Breakfast (Individual Rooms and applicable Self-Catering sub-types)

If breakfast is available, you can flag it here — we cover the full breakfast setup in Step 5.

If you're on Multi and manage several propertiesMake sure you're working inside the correct property before adding rooms — the property switcher is in Settings, and every room belongs to exactly one property.

Setting your rates and seasonal pricing
Go to Pricing, or Settings → Seasonal Pricing

Every room already has a base rate from Step 3 — that's the rate that applies by default, every night, unless you tell NestBook otherwise. Seasonal Pricing is how you tell it otherwise, for the periods where your prices genuinely change: a summer peak, a quiet winter rate, a premium week over Christmas and New Year.

Creating a rate period

A rate period is simply a date range with a price attached. You can set a period to repeat every year automatically (useful for a fixed "summer season" that's always July–August, for example) or set it as a one-off date range for a specific year.

Christmas doesn't trip this upIf your peak period crosses the new year — say, 24th December to 6th January — NestBook handles that correctly as one continuous period, rather than needing you to split it into two separate entries either side of January 1st.

Priority and overlaps

If two rate periods ever overlap on the same date, the one with higher priority wins. This is useful for carving out a short, higher-priced spike (like New Year's Eve itself) inside a longer, lower-priced season, without having to redraw the whole season around it.

Per-room overrides

By default, a rate period applies the same adjustment across every room. If one particular room should be priced differently during that period — your largest suite commanding a bigger premium at Christmas than your smallest room, for instance — you can set a specific override for just that room within the period.

PlanSeasonal rate periods
FreeNot available
ProUp to 5

Where guests see the effect of this

Whenever a guest selects dates — on your widget, your booking page, or when you create a booking manually — NestBook calculates the price night by night, showing exactly which nights fall under which rate. If a stay spans both a normal period and a seasonal period, the booking confirmation and the checkout will clearly break this down (for example: "3 nights × £95, 4 nights × £145 — Summer Peak"), so nobody, including you, has to work it out by hand.

Breakfast
Go to Settings → Breakfast Hours and your room settings
Whole-property ownersIf your property is a Whole Property rental (a gîte, cottage, or lodge), this step doesn't apply to you — guests self-cater, so breakfast settings are intentionally hidden throughout NestBook for your account. Feel free to skip ahead to Step 6.

If you do offer breakfast, there are three separate layers of control, so you can be as broad or as specific as you like:

Property-wide toggle

In Settings, there's a single toggle to offer breakfast to every guest at your property automatically, on every booking. Only switch this on if that's genuinely your policy — it applies across the board, to every room and every booking, with no exceptions.

Room-level breakfast

If breakfast varies by room type (perhaps only certain rooms include it), you can set this individually on each room in the Rooms section instead of using the property-wide toggle.

Booking-level breakfast

Even with the above two set, you can still add or remove breakfast on an individual booking — useful for a guest who's specifically requested it, or asked not to have it.

Wherever breakfast is included, it shows clearly to the guest — on the booking page, in their confirmation email, and as a distinctive dashed border on that booking's tile on your Calendar, so you can see at a glance which arriving guests are expecting it.

Deposits and getting paid
Go to Billing

This is one of the sections most owners ask about, so we've written it in full — take your time with it.

Deposit rules

Under Settings, you can decide exactly how you want to handle deposits, entirely on your own terms:

  • Whether to require one at all — this can be switched off completely if you'd rather not.
  • The type — a percentage of the total stay, a fixed amount, or the full amount upfront.
  • When the balance is due — for example, a set number of days before arrival.
  • Your refund policy — shown to guests so expectations are clear from the start.
  • Automatic emails — deposit requests, confirmations, and balance-due reminders can all be sent automatically, so you don't have to chase this by hand.

Once switched on, any new booking that needs a deposit will show a clear status (awaiting deposit, deposit received, balance due) both on the booking itself and in the side panel, so you always know where each guest stands financially at a glance.

Accepting real card payments — Stripe Connect All plans

NestBook lets you accept genuine card payments from guests, with the money going directly into your own bank account — not into NestBook's. We never touch your money, and we never take a cut of it. This is available on every plan, including Free.

Setting it up

  1. Go to Billing → Stripe Connect and click "Connect with Stripe."
  2. Fill in your business and personal details on Stripe's own secure page — this is Stripe's page, not ours, so your financial details are never seen or stored by NestBook.
  3. Stripe will then verify your details. Being honest with you: this isn't instant — it can take anywhere from a few minutes to a day or two, depending on what Stripe needs to check. You'll be brought back here automatically once it's done.
Why the wait, and why it's worth itStripe is legally required to verify who's actually receiving guest payments — this is standard practice for any business anywhere in the world accepting card payments, not something specific to NestBook. It only needs doing once.

Once connected, guests can pay you via payment links (sent directly from any booking), your booking widget ProMulti, and your standalone booking page. NestBook charges nothing for using Stripe Connect — you'll only pay Stripe's own standard card processing fee. You're free to disconnect at any time from the same Billing page.

Taking and managing bookings
Go to Calendar or Bookings

Adding a booking manually

Click "+ New Booking" from the Dashboard, Calendar, or Bookings screen. The form is built to be quick for repeat guests: start typing a guest's last name and NestBook will search your existing guest records first, so you're not re-typing details for someone who's stayed with you before.

Choose the dates first, and NestBook will show you which rooms are actually available for that exact date range — no need to check the calendar separately beforehand.

The Calendar

A week-by-week visual grid of every room and every booking. Each tile is colour-coded by status, and — if you've set up Seasonal Pricing — a small dot appears on any date with a special rate active, so you can see peak pricing at a glance without opening anything.

If you ever forget to check someone in or outNestBook will notice if a guest's check-in or check-out date has passed without you confirming it, and will show a friendly reminder on your Dashboard asking you to simply confirm what actually happened. Nothing gets stuck or lost if life gets busy.

Extending or shortening a stay

If a guest asks to stay extra nights, open their booking and choose to extend it — NestBook checks the new dates are actually free, works out the correct rate for the extra nights (respecting seasonal pricing if relevant), and gives you a clear breakdown before you confirm. An email is sent to the guest automatically with their new dates and updated total. Shortening a stay works the same way in reverse.

Guests and the booking journey
Go to Guests

Every person who books with you gets a guest record automatically — you never need to create one by hand unless you want to. Over time, this becomes a genuinely useful history: how many times someone has stayed, when they last visited, and their contact details, all in one place.

A typical booking's life, start to finish

  1. Booking made — by you directly, or by a guest through your widget or booking page.
  2. Confirmation email sent — automatically, in the guest's own registered language.
  3. Deposit requested (if you've enabled deposits) — with a reminder as the balance due date approaches.
  4. Check-in day — the booking status updates to "Arriving," and appears on your Dashboard's arrivals list.
  5. You confirm arrival — a simple tap moves the booking to "In house."
  6. Stay continues — you can add charges (Multi plan), breakfast, or handle an extension request at any point.
  7. Check-out day — you confirm departure, the room becomes available again, and if you use a cleaning workflow, a cleaning flag appears.

If a guest requests to book (rather than you entering it yourself)

When someone books through your widget or page and your settings require your approval, you'll receive an email with a simple Accept or Decline choice — and you can also action this directly inside NestBook from the booking's side panel, without needing to use the email link at all. The guest is automatically emailed the outcome either way.

Adding extras during a stay — Room Charges Multi

If you offer anything beyond the room itself — breakfast add-ons, a bar tab, an evening meal, bike hire, a spa treatment — the Room Charges screen lets you add these to a guest's account at any point during their stay, each with its own category and tax rate (set up in advance under Settings → Service Categories, covered in Step 12). Everything added shows as a running total on the guest's account, ready for a clean, itemised checkout when they leave.

Syncing with Booking.com and Airbnb
Go to Settings → Calendar Sync — available on every plan

If you also list your property on Booking.com, Airbnb, or similar platforms, the single biggest risk is a double booking — someone booking the same room for the same dates on two different platforms, because neither system knew about the other. iCal sync solves this.

How it works

Every room in NestBook has its own unique calendar link. Copy this link and paste it into Booking.com or Airbnb's own calendar-sync settings (both platforms have a section for this — look for "Sync calendars" or "iCal export/import" in their settings). Once connected, any booking made on that platform will automatically block those same dates in NestBook, and vice versa.

NestBook checks for updates from your connected external calendars every 15 minutes, so blocks stay current without you needing to do anything manually. Externally-blocked dates show clearly in grey on your calendar, so you can always tell at a glance whether a blocked date came from a NestBook booking or an external platform.

This is genuinely free, on every planUnlike some competitors, calendar sync is never gated behind a paid tier in NestBook — we think protecting you from double bookings shouldn't come with a price tag.

Full step-by-step instructions for connecting Booking.com and Airbnb specifically are also available on our Help page, since their own settings screens occasionally change slightly on their end.

Bringing in your existing data
Available from the Bookings, Rooms and Guests screens — on every plan

Moving to NestBook from another system? You don't need to re-type everything by hand. NestBook has migration wizards for your bookings, your rooms, and your guest list — each works from a CSV file (a simple spreadsheet format that Excel, Google Sheets, and most booking systems can export). The exact column order never matters; the importer matches by column heading, not position.

Bookings

From the Bookings screen, choose Import and upload a CSV containing guest name, email, phone, check-in and check-out dates, room, total price, and any notes. The wizard then walks you through three steps:

  1. Uploading your file — NestBook detects whether it's comma, semicolon, or tab-separated, and handles several different date formats without you needing to reformat anything first.
  2. Matching rooms — if your old system used different room names, a simple mapping step lines them up with your NestBook rooms. (Skipped in Whole Property mode, since there's only one to assign to.)
  3. Reviewing before anything is saved — you'll see exactly what's about to be imported, including duplicate-guest detection, so you're not creating a second record for someone already in your Guests list.

Imported bookings keep their original total, rather than NestBook trying to guess a nightly rate breakdown for a booking made before you were tracking rates this way.

Rooms and guests

The Rooms (or Units) screen has its own Import Rooms wizard — one version for each rental mode, chosen automatically for your setup — for bringing in your rooms, categories, or units from a CSV. The Guests screen has an Import option for your existing guest list. Both check the file first, flag anything that looks wrong, and let you fix it before confirming. As with bookings, nothing is saved until you approve it.

Marketing tools and your guest welcome pack
Three tools that help you look after guests and fill more rooms, without extra software

Property Info Sheet Pro

A genuinely useful one, especially for first-time guests: build a printable welcome pack for your property, combining whatever you want to include — your logo and property name, a WiFi QR code, check-in and check-out times, breakfast times and price, house rules, local directions, transport information, and recommendations for nearby shops, bars, and restaurants.

Write the content once — your house rules and local area guide are saved to your property and reused every time — then simply tick the sections you want included for a given export. Two ways to export it: as a print-ready PDF, or as a shareable image.

Social Media Kit Pro

Take the photos you've already uploaded to your rooms and turn them into ready-to-post content — NestBook generates a suggested caption for each photo, and gives you a one-click "Copy Image" or "Download" for posting directly to Facebook or Instagram. Alongside it, you'll find a plain-language guide to actually setting up and posting on Facebook and Instagram, written for someone who's never done it before.

Guest Mailer Pro

A way to send a genuinely branded email — with your own property name and logo — to guests who've stayed with you before. Useful for a seasonal offer, a reopening announcement, or simply staying in touch with people who already know and like your property.

Whose contact details you can useGuest Mailer works with any guest who gave you their email directly — through your booking page, your widget, over the phone, or in person at check-in. It doesn't matter how they originally found you. The one thing it can't be used for is emailing a contact address supplied only through Booking.com or Airbnb's own messaging systems, since neither platform allows their guest contact data to be used for outside marketing.
